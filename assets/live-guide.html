The live session · A guide for testing

How a live session runs, start to finish

The live session runs a two-hour client workshop with Lighthouse. Before the meeting, the client answers ten questions and Lighthouse digs four parts: Company, Competition, Customer and Climate. In the room, people decide what is true while Lighthouse listens and records. At the end, Lighthouse finds the countercurrents and writes the leave-behind, with a full report and the transcript.

Step 01 · Before the meeting

Create the client and a session

Where
The Live session page
  1. On the Live session page, fill in New client: the client's name, the brand, the category, the product and the market.
  2. Click Create the client.
  3. Click Start a new live session. The session's preparation page opens.

Step 02 · Before the meeting

Send the client their ten questions

Where
The preparation page, section 1
Who
The client, on a link of their own
  1. In section 1, Before the session: the client's questions, copy the link. The copy icon appears when you point at the box.
  2. Send it to the client. They need no password: ten questions, one per screen, and each answer is saved as they give it.
  3. For this test, open the link yourself and answer as if you were the client.
  4. Back on the preparation page, click Reload the answers to see them. You can fix an answer under Complete or correct the answers, then Save the answers.

Close the link stops the client from answering. Ending the session closes it too.

Step 03 · Before the meeting

Let Lighthouse dig the four parts

Where
The preparation page, sections 2 and 3
Time
About five minutes
  1. In section 2, What Lighthouse looks for, check the brand, category, market, competitors and the question. Click Save.
  2. In section 3, click Dig the four parts. It takes about five minutes, and you can keep working meanwhile.
  3. When it is done, read the currents in each part: Company, Competition, Customer and Climate.
  4. Click Cut on anything weak: it will not appear in the room. Use + Add a current for ideas of your own.
Company

Mission, marketing history, product and the challenge in front of it.

Competition

What competitors do, and what is moving in the wider industry.

Customer

What customers are dealing with, and the barriers they face.

Climate

Macro forces, and what is happening in culture right now.

Step 04 · In the room

Open the session screen

Where
The computer that will be in the meeting
  1. On the computer that will be in the meeting, click Open the session screen ›.
  2. The opening screen shows the run of show and what the client told us.
  3. On the right sits ◉ The room: the microphone, the people and the transcript. It is beside every part, and the recording starts there.
  4. The steps along the top move between the screens. Selects · 0 counts what the room has kept, and ‹ Prepare goes back to the preparation page, which stops the microphone.

Step 05 · In the room

Record the meeting

Lighthouse transcribes the meeting as it happens. That is how it notices which topics the room keeps coming back to, and how the final report gets its full transcript.

Where
The room panel, on the opening screen
Time
The 10′ intro
  1. Before anyone speaks, tell the room the conversation is being transcribed. Then click I've told the room ✓. The microphone stays off until you do.
  2. Click ▶︎ Listen and allow the microphone when the browser asks. The transcript appears in the room panel as people speak.
  3. In person: the laptop's microphone hears the room. Put the laptop in the middle of the table.
  4. On Google Meet: also click + Meet tab, choose the Meet tab and tick Also share tab audio. The other side is heard through the tab, your room through the microphone.
  5. Click Names to give each voice a name and a role. Mark the Atlantic people with Atlantic: their words do not count when Lighthouse picks. One person showing up as two voices? Join them with Same person as…
  6. Optional: click Round of introductions and ask everyone to say their name and role. Lighthouse names the voices for you. Click ■ End the introductions when everyone has spoken.
  7. Click Start · Company currents › to begin the first part.
Privacy

Lighthouse tells the voices apart by itself (Voice A, Voice B…). Nothing is kept as a voiceprint, and the audio is processed on AssemblyAI's servers in the EU.

Step 06 · In the room

Work through the four parts

Where
Company, Competition, Customer, Climate
Time
15′ each, with a 5′ break before Climate
  1. For each current, the room decides: ✓ Keep, ★ Star (the strongest), Tweak (rewrite it) or ✕ Kill. The keys K S T X decide the first current still open.
  2. Lighthouse listens, and reads the last minutes every 30 seconds. When people keep coming back to a topic, it marks the current ◉ Picked from the conversation. Click Undo if it got it wrong.
  3. Prefer to approve each pick? Switch the room panel to AI suggests, then click Confirm or Dismiss on each suggestion.
  4. When someone disagrees, Lighthouse only flags the current as Contested. It never kills anything on its own.
  5. Ask Lighthouse: type a question in the box, such as “what about Gen Z planners?”, and click Dig ›. It comes back with a new current and its evidence. Their questions, under the box, are the client's own: click one to dig it.
  6. Add an idea: type it in the + Add a current box and click Add. Lighthouse looks for posts that back it.
  7. Move between parts with the steps at the top, or with ‹ Back and Next · Competition › at the bottom of each part.

Step 07 · In the room

Selects and countercurrents

Where
Selects, then Countercurrent
Time
5′, then 30′
  1. Click Selects at the top: everything the room kept, side by side. You need at least four. Take it out removes one.
  2. Click Find the countercurrents ›. Lighthouse proposes territories in the gaps between what the room kept, and starred currents carry more weight.
  3. Discuss them. Write the room's notes in each one, and click Mark ripe on the good ones.
  4. Click Regenerate with the notes. Ripe territories stay; the others are rewritten using the notes.

Step 08 · After

The leave-behind and the final report

Where
Brief, the last step at the top
Time
The 10′ of next steps
  1. Click Write the leave-behind ›. It is the brief built together in the room: the challenge, the territories and the evidence behind them.
  2. Click Save it and get a link to share it with the client, no password needed. Export PDF opens the print dialog: choose Save as PDF.
  3. Under The final report, click Show the full report. It holds everything: the client's answers, every current and who decided it, every question to Lighthouse, every interaction with its time, and the full transcript.
  4. Untick the transcript or the timeline if a copy should leave them out. What is ticked goes into the link and the PDF, so after a change click Save the changes to the link. Download the transcript (.txt) gives the transcript on its own.
  5. Click End the session when you are done. The microphone stops and the client's question link closes.
  6. Later, on the preparation page (‹ Prepare), section 5 has the report PDF, the transcript and the link again.

If something goes wrong

What the screen says, and what to do

The room panelMicrophone blocked.
Allow the microphone for this site in the browser's address bar, then press ▶︎ Listen again.
The room panelNo sound came with the tab.
Click + Meet tab again, choose the Meet tab and tick Also share tab audio.
The room panelLost the connection to AssemblyAI.
Press ▶︎ Listen to try again. If it keeps failing, click Use the free mode: the browser transcribes and you tap who is speaking, with the name buttons or the keys 1 to 9. The free mode does not hear the Meet tab, and in Chrome the audio goes to Google's speech service. The same goes for AssemblyAI did not accept the connection.
The top of the screenNot saved — retrying
Keep going. The change is kept on the page and saved with the next one. Do not reload the page while it shows.
The preparation pageSomeone has this session open on another screen.
One screen drives a session: close it on the other computer. Two screens overwrite each other's changes.
